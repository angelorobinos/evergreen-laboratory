#### "Clean Energy" and the Normalization of Invisible Suffering

Reading Cobalt Red forced me to recalibrate what I thought “unlucky” meant. What happens in the Congo is not random misfortune—it is a system of modern slavery deliberately built to power the modern world. Children and adults risk their lives mining cobalt so the rest of us can enjoy smartphones, electric vehicles, and “clean” energy with a clear conscience.  
  
What makes this book unsettling is how directly it implicates the reader. Every device we charge is quietly connected to suffering that has been pushed out of sight, buried deep in global supply chains. The true horror is not only the violence in the mines, but how normal and invisible it has become.  

After reading this book, privilege feels painfully concrete. Cobalt Red does not offer comfort; it demands responsibility. If we continue to rely on lithium-ion battery–powered electronics, we must at least use them more consciously—buy fewer devices, keep them longer, repair instead of replace, and support companies that are transparent about ethical sourcing. Awareness alone is not enough; changing how we consume technology is the minimum moral response.
