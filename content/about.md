# About

Evergreen Laboratory is a digital garden for ideas that benefit from time.

Most writing on the internet is designed for immediacy: short posts, quick reactions, and content that disappears into the stream after a few days. This site follows a different rhythm. It collects notes, essays, and research reflections that are meant to grow, connect, and occasionally be revisited years later.

The name *Evergreen Laboratory* reflects two ideas.

**Evergreen** refers to knowledge that remains useful over time. Instead of writing only for the present moment, the goal here is to develop ideas that can mature into durable concepts.

**Laboratory** reflects the experimental nature of the project. Many notes begin as small observations or questions. Some evolve into longer essays or more stable explanations. Others remain fragments that may eventually connect with something else.

The site contains three main kinds of writing:

- **Essays** – longer pieces on science, technology, habit, and the moral texture of everyday life.  
- **Research notes** – reflections from work in materials science, particularly lignin-derived carbon materials and sodium-ion batteries.  
- **Evergreen notes** – compact ideas and conceptual fragments designed to be linked, revisited, and gradually refined.

## About the author

I am a doctoral researcher based in Finland working on lignin-derived hard carbon materials for sodium-ion batteries.

My research explores how renewable materials such as lignin can be transformed into functional carbon structures for energy storage. Alongside laboratory work, I am interested in the broader craft of scientific thinking: how ideas develop, how knowledge compounds, and how complex systems can be understood through careful observation.

Outside of research, my interests include knowledge systems, digital gardening, writing, and the slow process of building ideas that remain useful over time.

Evergreen Laboratory is where these interests meet.
